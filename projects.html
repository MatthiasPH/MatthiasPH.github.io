PROJECT 01 / 07ROBOTICS / EMBEDDED SYSTEMS

Quadruped Robot — Version 2.

I redesigned my first walking robot into an eight-servo quadruped with independently controlled hip and knee joints. An ESP32-C3 coordinates the gait, while onboard sensing provides a platform for obstacle detection and future autonomous behaviours.

THE CHALLENGE

Coordinate two joints per leg while keeping the chassis and electronics practical to assemble, calibrate and debug.

WHAT I DID

Developed multi-servo gait sequences, calibrated servo centres and travel limits, and refined power distribution and cable routing.

THE OUTCOME

Assembled a more capable second-version platform with greater control over foot placement, body motion and future sensor experiments.

PROJECT 02 / 07MECHANICAL DESIGN / DRIVETRAIN

Custom RC Car.

I designed and built an RC car with a 3D-printed chassis and electric drivetrain. The work combined CAD, gear calculations, printed gearbox and motor mounts, shaft support, assembly and repeated physical testing.

THE CHALLENGE

Package a compact geartrain while keeping shafts aligned, moving parts supported and friction under control.

WHAT I DID

Calculated gear ratios and centre distances, worked through backlash and bearing support, and revised printed mounts based on assembly tests.

THE OUTCOME

A modular drivetrain prototype that made the effects of tolerances, stiffness and print orientation visible in real hardware.

PROJECT 03 / 07PARAMETRIC CAD / MANUFACTURING

Modular Dowel Storage Mounts.

I developed a family of printed mounts for 20 mm wooden dowels, allowing the same parts to support different modular storage and bracing arrangements. The design is inexpensive, replaceable and adaptable.

THE CHALLENGE

Achieve reliable locating and holding fits despite dimensional variation in FDM-printed components.

WHAT I DID

Iterated small fit-test pieces, varied clearances and print orientation, and used wall thickness, fillets and braces to improve the load path.

THE OUTCOME

Repeatable mount geometries with practical slip and interference fit tolerances, reducing wasted prints during development.

PROJECT 04 / 07SOFTWARE / ELECTRONICS / FABRICATION

Warman Design Challenge.

For our university Warman project, the challenge was to deliver two balls at different heights over an obstacle into a small target, using an Arduino and four DC motors. My main contributions were the electronics, C programming and 3D-printed fabrication. I also developed an alternative CAD concept, which wasn't selected for the team's final build.

THE CHALLENGE

Build a reliable ball-delivery mechanism under tight motor and controller constraints while keeping the prototype light and practical to manufacture.

WHAT I DID

Integrated the electronics onto a smaller PCB, wrote the core motor-control functions in C and programmed concurrent motor operation within current limits. I also fabricated parts with 3D printing and compared materials to balance weight and strength.

THE OUTCOME

A working team prototype with compact control electronics and coordinated motor operation to reduce task time. My separate CAD concept also strengthened my understanding of mechanical integration and design for assembly.

PROJECT 05 / 07CAD / 3D-PRINTED PROTOTYPE

Modular Two-Stage Rocket.

I designed the first iteration of a small modular two-stage rocket in Fusion 360 and assembled a full-size printed prototype. The first stage uses fins for passive stability; gyroscope-controlled thrust vectoring was a proposed second-stage concept, not an implemented control system.

THE CHALLENGE

Create repeatable interfaces and package nested sections while balancing stability concepts with manufacturability.

WHAT I DID

Modelled the modular components, tuned mating clearances and checked physical fit and scale with printed assemblies.

THE OUTCOME

A full-size prototype that exposed tolerance stack-up and assembly issues ahead of any advanced sensing or control development.

PROJECT 06 / 07ARDUINO / PRODUCT PROTOTYPING

Automatic Rubbish Bin.

I built a touch-free rubbish bin for my 3D-printing workspace using an Arduino, an IR proximity sensor, a display and an SG90 servo. Detection triggers the lid, which stays open for approximately two seconds before closing.

THE CHALLENGE

Make a reliable hands-free mechanism using simple electronics, while avoiding missed detections and unwanted triggers.

WHAT I DID

Integrated sensing, display and actuation using non-blocking timing; refined sensor placement, servo travel and enclosure access.

THE OUTCOME

A compact practical prototype that applies embedded control to a real workspace problem: containing 3D-printing waste.

PROJECT 07 / 07ELECTRONICS / INTEGRATION

Modular Electronics Control Board.

I designed and soldered a compact, modular board bringing together an Arduino Uno, two motor-driver modules, a 5 V buck converter and removable connections. It replaced a larger, loose wiring arrangement.

THE CHALLENGE

Distribute regulated power and route control signals cleanly while keeping higher-current motor drivers replaceable.

WHAT I DID

Planned electrical integration and connector access, soldered the board, and used continuity checks and multimeter testing to isolate faults.

THE OUTCOME

A neater, serviceable control assembly with clearer cable management and easier troubleshooting during prototyping.
