ABOUT ME / THE PROCESS

Curious about
how things work.

I'm Matthias, a Mechanical Engineering student at the University of Auckland who enjoys designing, analysing, building and—probably most importantly—testing things.

I'm interested in the gap between what a design is supposed to do and what it actually does once it exists in the real world. That gap is where I learn the most: adjusting geometry, refining mechanisms, tracing faults and understanding why an idea didn't work quite as planned.

01 / MAKING THINGS

From screen
to workbench.

3D printing plays a big role in how I approach design. I enjoy the speed of starting with a model, making a physical component and finding out almost immediately whether it behaves as intended. Instead of stopping at a render, I can check assembly fit, clearances, stiffness, print quality and how parts interact with the rest of a mechanism.

Through that process I've become interested in the small details that matter in manufacturing: print orientation, support strategies, wall thickness, infill, material selection, dimensional accuracy and designing around tolerances. Sometimes the right solution is changing a dimension by a fraction of a millimetre. Other times it means redesigning the whole interface.

That same iterative mindset carries over to my other projects, from robotics and RC systems to fixing electronic hardware. I like working between disciplines—mechanical design, electronics and software—because the challenge is rarely limited to just one.
